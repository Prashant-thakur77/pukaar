## Anticipatory cash transfers (why pay before the flood)

- Oxford CSAE / WFP, Bangladesh 2020 floods: $53 by mobile money to 23,000+ households about a week before the flood peak; recipients 52% less likely to go a day without eating; children more likely to have eaten three meals; lower asset loss; higher earning potential at 3 months; stated wellbeing 18.7% higher. https://csae.web.ox.ac.uk/node/2183966
- Working paper: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:12ea16b2-edc0-4af5-8824-132aba4557bd
- IPA anticipatory action brief (Sept 2026): https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/IPA-BRIEF-Anticipatory%20Action%20Brief-r5_1554.pdf
- Trial registry: https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/6576
- Anticipation Hub evidence database: https://www.anticipation-hub.org/experience/evidence-database/evidence-list

## Blockchain cash transfers in the field (what worked, what did not)

- Oxfam UnBlocked Cash, Vanuatu 2019 (DAI + NFC via Sempo): onboarding ~3.6 min/person vs >1 h in the 2018 Ambae response; 187 households, 29 vendors, ~1,209 people; direct transaction costs did not clearly fall because cash-out still depended on local financial providers. https://reliefweb.int/report/vanuatu/unblocked-cash-piloting-accelerated-cash-transfer-delivery-vanuatu-research-report · https://www.oxfam.org/en/node/16213
- WFP Building Blocks: https://www.wfp.org/building-blocks
- Critique of single-operator "blockchains" (why Raahat uses three independent attesters and a public chain): https://davidgerard.co.uk/blockchain/2017/11/26/the-world-food-programmes-much-publicised-blockchain-has-one-participant-i-e-its-a-database/
