In 2025, a dog woke a Himalayan village at midnight. No warning reached them. And when relief comes, nobody can see where it goes. This is Pukaar Raahat.

On the night of 30 June 2025, cloudbursts hit Mandi district: at least 10 dead, 34 missing. Relief is paid after the water, and the village can't check who got it.

Money before the water works. In Bangladesh in 2020, the World Food Programme sent $53 by mobile money, about a week before the flood peaked, to over 23,000 households. They were 52% less likely to go a day without eating.

Today, Pukaar warns villages before a flood, live on AWS. Raahat adds the money: released before the river, on a sealed rule, every rupee on a public chain.

The rule is set before the monsoon: at Warning, ₹1,500 per household; once the flood is confirmed, ₹4,000 more; then a 72-hour cooldown. Then it's sealed on chain. Nobody can change it mid-flood, not even us.

Now July 2023, replayed on the deployed stack, labelled as an exercise. Pandoh reaches Warning. The officer approves the Hindi alert in one tap.

As it goes out, the alert is anchored on Base. Re-verify fetches the public alert feed and hashes it in your browser. It matches.

Code checks the sealed rule: Pandoh is at Warning, 30 households registered. One tap releases tier one, before the water: 30 times ₹1,500. The contract checks the rule again, and pays.

After the flood, three independent keys can confirm it. Pandoh's pradhan attests first, from a verified photo of the village.

The district officer attests, severity two. Two of three agree: the flood is confirmed. And the satellite adds a third.

Sentinel-2 images of the river, before and after: water went from 2.07 to 4.10 square kilometres. Severity three. The image's hash goes on chain, so anyone can check it.

Confirmation unlocks tier two: ₹4,000 for each household. The contract checks the three keys itself.

The pradhan records each payment's UPI reference. On chain, it's a receipt for a hashed household: no name, no phone number.

It all lands on one public page: the alert, both tranches, three confirmations, the receipts.

Anyone can open every transaction: no wallet, no account.

Why a chain? A rule nobody can quietly change, a confirmation no one person controls, receipts anyone can audit. And if the chain is down, the warning still goes out.

What's not real yet: the money is a test token. The amounts await the state's relief norms, and our server holds the testnet keys.
